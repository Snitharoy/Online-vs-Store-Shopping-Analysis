# Online vs Store Shopping Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes customer shopping behavior and compares **Online, Store, and Hybrid** shopping preferences. The analysis focuses on customer demographics, spending behavior, shopping activity, digital engagement, payment trust, and other behavioral factors.

The project uses Python-based data analytics techniques to identify patterns and generate meaningful business insights that can support customer targeting, marketing decisions, and retail strategy.

## Problem Statement

The growth of e-commerce and digital technologies has given customers multiple ways to shop through online platforms and physical stores. Businesses therefore need to understand how customer characteristics and shopping behavior differ across shopping channels.

This project analyzes the **Online vs Store Shopping Dataset** to identify differences in customer preferences, purchasing patterns, spending behavior, and digital engagement across Online, Store, and Hybrid shoppers.

## Project Objectives

- Analyze customer shopping behavior.
- Compare Online, Store, and Hybrid shopping patterns.
- Perform data quality checks and preprocessing.
- Conduct Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA).
- Identify relationships between different variables.
- Visualize customer purchasing trends.
- Generate meaningful business insights.
- Provide data-driven recommendations.

## Dataset

The dataset contains **11,789 customer records and 25 features** related to demographics, shopping behavior, spending, payment methods, digital engagement, and shopping preferences.

The main shopping preference categories analyzed are:

- **Online**
- **Store**
- **Hybrid**

The dataset includes both numerical and categorical variables, making it suitable for data cleaning, preprocessing, exploratory analysis, aggregation, and visualization.

## Key Insights

- **Store shopping is the dominant preference**, with 10,244 customers, followed by Online with 1,176 and Hybrid with 369 customers.
- Store customers have the **highest average store spending**, at approximately ₹85,102.73.
- Store customers also have the **highest average total spending**, at approximately ₹159,451.55.
- Online customers have the **highest average online spending**, at approximately ₹76,428.96.
- Online customers place the **highest average number of monthly online orders**, at approximately 28.31.
- Store customers have the **highest average monthly store visits**, at approximately 9.63.
- Tech-savviness, monthly online orders, online payment trust, and daily internet usage show positive associations with Online shopping preference.
- Average store spending shows the strongest negative association with Online shopping preference in the correlation analysis.
- Need-to-touch-and-feel score and monthly store visits also show negative associations with Online shopping preference.
- Most demographic variables show little or no strong relationship with shopping preference in this dataset.

> **Note:** Correlation indicates association and does not establish causation.

## Business Recommendations

- Focus on customer retention and in-store engagement because Store customers form the largest segment and have the highest average total spending.
- Improve online customer engagement through payment trust, repeat-purchase strategies, and suitable digital promotions.
- Encourage Hybrid customers to use both Online and Store channels through integrated offers and promotions.
- Use behavioral variables such as spending, online orders, store visits, internet usage, and payment trust for customer targeting.
- Test delivery-fee and return-policy strategies to understand their relationship with online order activity.
- Develop personalized marketing campaigns for different shopping segments.
- Use predictive analytics in the future to identify customers who are likely to prefer Online, Store, or Hybrid shopping.

## Project Conclusion

The analysis of 11,789 customer records provides insights into the differences between Online, Store, and Hybrid shoppers. Store shopping is the dominant preference and Store customers generate the highest average total spending in the dataset. Online customers show stronger digital shopping activity, including higher online order frequency and online spending.

The analysis also indicates that behavioral and digital-engagement variables provide useful signals for understanding shopping preference. Overall, the project demonstrates how data cleaning, preprocessing, aggregation, visualization, and exploratory analysis can transform customer data into actionable business insights.
